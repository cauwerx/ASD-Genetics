# Autism Sequencing Consortium Flagship manuscript
Code repository for *"Rare variation illuminates the distinct and pleiotropic genetic architecture of autism"*

**DOI:** [Preprint](ATO ADD)

**Contact:** TO ADD.

**Summary:** 
Autism spectrum disorder is a highly heterogeneous trait that shares features with other neurodevelopmental and neuropsychiatric disorders. Here, we aggregate exome sequence data from 62,429 individuals with autism to explore its genetic architecture and investigate overlap with other phenotypes. We use a Bayesian framework to integrate information across variant classes and inheritance modes to identify 253 autosomal genes where damaging mutations confer risk for autism at a false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.001. Genes lower on the list exhibit an increasing proportion of evidence from missense and inherited variants on average, reflecting genes of more moderate penetrance. Stratifying our cohort by co-occurring global developmental delay and/or intellectual disability (DD/ID) identifies subsets of genes enriched for variants in autism with or without DD/ID, while jointly analyzing our cohort with 26,109 samples independently ascertained for developmental disorder identifies 480 genes that broadly impact neurodevelopment (FDR < 0.001). Clustering the 253 autism-associated genes based on association evidence across separate studies of developmental disorder, schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, and epilepsy generates six clusters; we observe a gradation of phenotypic effects among autistic individuals who have a de novo variant in clusters more associated with developmental disorder, in the remaining clusters, or in none. Finally, leveraging UK Biobank and All of Us data, we characterize the pleiotropic effects of autism-associated genes across diverse human phenotypes, identifying highly pleiotropic genes affecting a broad set of physiological systems.

## Data availability: 
De novo variant calls and most data generated in this paper are available in Supplementary Tables 1-35. Variant-level counts are further available on http://asc.broadinstitute.org. Access to raw sequencing data is subject to the policies of the originating study or repository and may have controlled access to protect participant privacy. Raw Autism Sequencing Consortium data is provided through NHGRI AnVIL: https://anvilproject.org/data, accession IDs phs000298 and phs002502; raw SPARK data is available from the Simons Foundation Autism Research Initiative, SFARI Base: https://www.sfari.org/resource/spark, dataset iWES_v2; access to UKB is through the UKB platform under application 31063; GeneDx data is provided through collaboration and partnership with GeneDx.
